Comments and The manuscript proposes Causal Fingerprinting Oracle (CFO), a two-phase consensusmechanism in which smart contracts first generate controlled perturbations and collectagent response differentials Ay = f(x + Ax) f(x), and then apply spectral analysis to thecovariance matrix of these fingerprints to identify structurally inconsistent agents, with thecentral claim that this enables empirical Byzantine tolerance up to 40% while maintainingabout 94.1% +4.9% accuracy and ~3.5 s latency in a 10-agent setting. This is anoriginal and interesting idea because it reframes consensus from output agreement toreasoning-coherence verification, and the paper does a good job positioning CFO againsttrust-aware, game-theoretic, learning-based, and ZKP-based mechanisms. However,several issues need to be addressed more rigorously: the security expression Psuccess (1/Aj x NxL and the cumulative strength equation are presented as if they were formalresults, but the manuscript does not provide a sufficiently rigorous derivation or explicitprobabilistic assumptions; the experimental validation is based on only 50 queries acrossprediction, reasoning, and classification tasks, which is too limited to convincingly supportbroad claims about blockchain-oracle robustness; the agents are all implemented with theDeep Seek API (deepseek-chat) and differ mainly through prompt variation, whichveakens the claim of heterogeneity; and the comparison with PBFT, Hotstuff,Tendermint, and BFT-SMaRt is largely tabular and conceptual rather than based oncontrolled empirical benchmarking under matched attack settings. I also encourage theauthors to include an ablation study isolating the contribution of perturbation, covarianceconstruction, eigenvalue decomposition, spectral entropy, and the cosine-similarity sieve(0 = 0.85); to provide a clearer complexity analysis beyond the stated O(n2), especiallygiven the cost of multiple perturbation rounds (K= 5), on-chain archival, and spectraldecomposition; and to moderate the interpretation of the "beyond 33% BFT" claim bydistinguishing clearly between theoretical guarantees and empirical observations in thisspecific experimental environment. Overall, the paper has a strong and timely core idea,but it needs substantially stronger theoretical grounding, broader experimentation, andmore careful calibration of its claims.
